\begin{abstract}
We model state of charge by energy conservation,
\(E_{\mathrm{eff}}\dot{\mathrm{SOC}}=-P(t)\).
Under constant load the time-to-empty is \(t_{\mathrm{empty}}=E_{\mathrm{eff}}\mathrm{SOC}_0/P\).
A known-answer instance (\(15\,\mathrm{Wh}\), \(3\,\mathrm{W}\), full charge) gives \(5\,\mathrm{h}\),
and Euler integration matches the linear solution.
Relative elasticities of \(t_{\mathrm{empty}}\) with respect to additive power components equal
minus their power shares, so the largest load---here the assumed screen term---dominates.
The same two loads in opposite order change time-to-empty from \(31\,\mathrm{h}\) to \(3.45\,\mathrm{h}\).
All numerical powers are order-of-magnitude assumptions, not an open dataset.
This draft does not claim a finished contest paper.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The official task is to build a continuous-time lithium-ion model that returns \(\mathrm{SOC}(t)\)
and predicts time-to-empty under realistic use.
Data may support parameter estimation but cannot replace an explicit continuous-time law.
The PDF text layer is empty (glyphs are drawn as paths); we work from a PDFTomd transcript
and leave one OCR-garbled clause in Requirement~1 unmarked.

\section{Assumptions and Justifications}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Lumped SOC only (ASM-0001). A Doyle--Fuller--Newman model is not identifiable here.
\item Energy balance \(E_{\mathrm{eff}}\dot{\mathrm{SOC}}=-P\) (ASM-0002). This is the definition of SOC in continuous time.
\item Additive power: idle, screen, CPU, network, GPS, background (ASM-0003).
\item Temperature and aging enter only as \(E_{\mathrm{eff}}=E_{\mathrm{nom}}\eta(T)\alpha\) (ASM-0004).
\item No charging on a time-to-empty interval (ASM-0005).
\item Current numbers are assumed magnitudes, not licensed measurements (ASM-0006).
\end{enumerate}

\section{The Model}
Why this model: SOC is remaining energy over effective capacity.
Differentiating the definition yields
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm{eff}}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathrm{SOC}(t)=-P(t),\qquad
P(t)=\sum_{i}P_i(t).
\end{equation}
If \(P\) is constant and positive,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SOC}(t)=\mathrm{SOC}_0-\frac{P}{E_{\mathrm{eff}}}t,\qquad
t_{\mathrm{empty}}=\frac{E_{\mathrm{eff}}\mathrm{SOC}_0}{P}.
\end{equation}
Black-box regression is excluded by the problem statement.
An equivalent-circuit model is deferred until open \(I\)--\(V\) traces exist (DEC-0001).

\section{Solution and Results}
Known-answer check: \(E_{\mathrm{eff}}=15\,\mathrm{Wh}\), \(P=3\,\mathrm{W}\), \(\mathrm{SOC}_0=1\)
gives \(t_{\mathrm{empty}}=5\,\mathrm{h}\) and \(\mathrm{SOC}(2.5\,\mathrm{h})=0.5\).
Euler with 200 steps reproduces the second value.
Idle power \(0.3\,\mathrm{W}\) alone gives \(50\,\mathrm{h}\).
These identities are internal to the ODE (CLM-0001 / EVD-0001).

\section{Sensitivity and Error Analysis}
For \(t_{\mathrm{empty}}\propto 1/P\) and \(P=\sum P_i\),
\begin{equation}
\frac{P_i}{t_{\mathrm{empty}}}\frac{\partial t_{\mathrm{empty}}}{\partial P_i}=-\frac{P_i}{P}.
\end{equation}
The ranking of drivers is therefore the ranking of power shares and does not depend on
\(E_{\mathrm{eff}}\) or \(\mathrm{SOC}_0\) (DEC-0002).
On the assumed baseline \((0.3,1.5,0.8,0.2,0.1,0.1)\,\mathrm{W}\),
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
component & share & elasticity \\
\midrule
screen & $0.50$ & $-0.50$ \\
CPU & $0.267$ & $-0.267$ \\
idle & $0.10$ & $-0.10$ \\
network & $0.067$ & $-0.067$ \\
GPS & $0.033$ & $-0.033$ \\
background & $0.033$ & $-0.033$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
A \(20\%\) increase in screen power moves TTE from \(5\,\mathrm{h}\) to \(15/3.3\approx 4.55\,\mathrm{h}\).
Capacity derating \(\eta=0.75\) or \(\alpha=0.85\) scales every TTE by the same factor
(\(3.75\,\mathrm{h}\) and \(4.25\,\mathrm{h}\)) and does not reorder the power shares.
If each \(P_i\) independently has a \(20\%\) relative error (assumed, not measured),
\(\sigma_P/P\approx 0.12\); with a further \(10\%\) error on \(E_{\mathrm{eff}}\),
a first-order RSS bound is \(\sigma_t/t\approx 0.15\).

The same ODE with piecewise-constant \(P(t)\) answers usage-order questions (DEC-0003).
For \(E_{\mathrm{eff}}=15\,\mathrm{Wh}\), a heavy block of \(6\,\mathrm{W}\) for one hour
followed by \(0.3\,\mathrm{W}\) idle empties at \(31\,\mathrm{h}\);
the swapped order empties at \(3.45\,\mathrm{h}\).
Order is not a second-order correction: it changes time-to-empty by about nine times
because the heavy block either spends leftover energy slowly or finishes the pack quickly.
A constant \(3\,\mathrm{W}\) day (\(5\,\mathrm{h}\)) cannot see this effect.

The model is weak wherever voltage efficiency, Peukert rate-capacity, or thermal coupling
makes \(P\) non-additive or \(E_{\mathrm{eff}}\) load-dependent.

\section{Strengths and Weaknesses}
Strengths: explicit continuous-time law; analytical TTE; falsifiable linear-SOC prediction;
component ranking that answers the problem's ``which activities'' question.
Weaknesses: no open licensed drain dataset; parameters are assumed; no voltage dynamics;
Requirement~1 still contains an unread OCR gap.

\section{Conclusion}
Under the energy-balance model, cut the largest power share first---on our assumed baseline,
that is screen brightness---and do not expect tiny background tasks to move TTE much.
An operating system can implement the same ranking: estimate component powers, form shares,
and throttle the largest share first; derate \(E_{\mathrm{eff}}\) when cold or aged.
Schedule high-draw work when a charger will be available soon: under energy conservation,
doing the heavy block last can empty the pack during that block.
The same ODE applies to any portable pack if loads can be treated as additive.
These recommendations inherit ASM-0006: they are mechanism translations, not field trials.
